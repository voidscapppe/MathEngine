Calculus III — Theory


        


            

                
Partial Derivatives

                
Differentiate a function with respect to one
                   variable while keeping other variables constant.

                
fₓ, fᵧ, fₓₓ, fᵧᵧ, fₓᵧ

            


            

                
Gradient

                
The gradient points in the direction
                   of steepest increase.

                
∇f = (fₓ, fᵧ, fᵤ)

                
For three variables x, y and z, the third component is fᶻ (∂f/∂z).

            


            

                
Directional Derivative

                
Rate of change along a unit vector u.

                
Dᵤf = ∇f · u

            


            

                
Hessian Matrix

                
Second-order derivatives used for
                   stationary point classification.

                
D = fₓₓfᵧᵧ − (fₓᵧ)²

            


            

                
Lagrange Multipliers

                
Optimization subject to constraints.

                
∇f = λ∇g

            


            

                
Double Integrals

                
Signed volume and integration
                   over two-dimensional regions.

                
∫∫ f(x,y) dA

            


            

                
Triple Integrals

                
Integration over three-dimensional regions.

                
∫∫∫ f(x,y,z) dV

            


            

                
Jacobian

                
Change of variables in multiple integrals.

                
J = det(∂(x,y)/∂(u,v))

            


            

                
Vector Fields

                
A vector associated with each point in space.

                
F = Pi + Qj + Rk

            


            

                
Divergence and Curl

                
Measure local expansion and rotation.

                
∇ · F, ∇ × F

            


            

                
Line and Surface Integrals

                
Integration along curves and surfaces.

                
∫ F · dr, ∬ F · n dS

            


            

                
Green, Stokes and Gauss

                
Fundamental theorems connecting integrals
                   over regions with integrals over their boundaries.
